# Voice and tone

The current voice is compact, specific, and house-led. It talks like a place where people build, share context, and keep moving.

## Current voice principles

- Favor short lines over explanatory paragraphs.
- Keep the tone warm, direct, and grounded in the house.
- Use the site language that already exists, including "a house for builders with taste," "live close to the work," and `house protocol`.
- Let the copy feel lived in, not branded.

## Do

- Use plain descriptions of people, work, and visits.
- Keep section headings tight and concrete.
- Make contact copy direct and useful.
- Use the resident and guestbook data to carry detail instead of adding extra explanation.

Examples:

- "a house for builders with taste"
- "live close to the work"
- "ship more than you explain"
- "leave your internet card"
- "want to build from here?"

## Do not

- Do not use generic startup language.
- Do not use coworking space language.
- Do not use accelerator language.
- Do not use corporate innovation language.
- Do not over-explain the vibe.

## Page copy

- Keep hero copy simple and specific.
- Reuse existing phrases when they already fit the point.
- Avoid introducing a new voice just to make a section feel different.

## Resident entries

- Keep bios concise and descriptive.
- Describe what the person builds, studies, or ships.
- Keep labels and roles short, such as `Founder Resident` or `Builder Resident`.

## Guestbook entries

- Keep guestbook notes short and personal.
- Focus on what the person is building or what they left behind.
- Preserve the "trace" and "guest entry" style already used on the site.

## Contact copy

- Keep contact text direct.
- Say what someone should do next.
- Avoid formal invite language and avoid over-selling access.
